How Culture Seeker works, and where it falls short

Culture Seeker draws on onomastics, the study of names. It holds a reference set of about 4,500 surnames and 3,000 given names across 62 traditions, along with about 80 spelling patterns that linguists and genealogists use to place names, such as Polish -ski, Armenian -yan, Gaelic Mac-, Finnish -nen and Georgian -shvili. When a name isn't in the reference set, Culture Seeker compares its spelling with the closest known names and says so in the results, with lower confidence. Each origin carries a population profile of religious affiliation drawn from widely published survey figures (Pew Research Center, national censuses). The percentages are model weights, not measured probabilities.

A name records the language and tradition of the family that passed it on. It does not record a person's own beliefs, citizenship, or full ancestry. Surnames usually follow one line of descent, people convert or leave religions, and names change through marriage, migration and adoption. Use these results as a starting point for curiosity and conversation. They are not a basis for decisions about anyone.

Everything runs in your browser. Names you type are not sent or stored anywhere.
