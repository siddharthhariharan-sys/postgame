How Culture Seeker works, and where it falls short

Culture Seeker draws on onomastics, the study of names. It holds a reference set of about 4,500 surnames and 3,000 given names across 66 traditions, along with about 80 spelling patterns that linguists and genealogists use to place names, such as Polish -ski, Armenian -yan, Gaelic Mac-, Finnish -nen and Georgian -shvili. When a name isn't in the reference set, Culture Seeker compares its spelling with the closest known names and says so in the results, with lower confidence. Each origin carries a population profile of religious affiliation drawn from widely published survey figures (Pew Research Center, national censuses). The percentages are model weights, not measured probabilities.

A name records the language and tradition of the family that passed it on. It does not record a person's own beliefs, citizenship, or full ancestry. Surnames usually follow one line of descent, people convert or leave religions, and names change through marriage, migration and adoption. Use these results as a starting point for curiosity and conversation. They are not a basis for decisions about anyone.

Sources. The reference set combines curated lists of surnames and given names with the person-name data from the open-source Faker library (MIT licence), which holds about 35,000 additional names grouped by country. Where your viewer allows it, Claude also gives its own assessment, drawing on what it learned from the published literature on names. Claude can't browse the web from this page and can be wrong, especially about rare names.

The reference-data estimate runs entirely in your browser. Where Claude's assessment is shown, the name you look up is also sent to Claude on your own Claude account. Nothing is stored by this page.
